99 (NINETY-NINE) — RULES
Source: Bicycle Cards, https://bicyclecards.com/how-to-play/99-ninety-nine

PLAYERS: 3 or 4.
THE PACK: A standard 52-card deck (no Jokers). Plus 3 tokens for each player
(pennies, buttons, poker chips, etc.).

OBJECT OF THE GAME
Players place cards into a pile in the middle, counting up a running point
total as they go. Go over 99 and you lose a token and the round is over. The
game ends when only one player has a token remaining.

THE DEAL
Each player places their three tokens in front of them and receives three
cards from the dealer. The running total starts at zero.

THE PLAY
Play goes around the table; on your turn you put down one card.
Each card adds its face value in points (a 5 is worth five points; a face card
is worth 10 points) EXCEPT for these special cards:
  - A 4 reverses the direction of play, and does not change the total.
  - A 9 is a pass, and does not change the total.
  - A 10 subtracts 10 points from the total.
  - A King takes the total to 99 (or keeps it at 99 if it is already 99).
  - An Ace adds either 1 or 11 points, announced by the player who plays it.
After playing a card, you announce the new total to the table and draw a
replacement card (so your hand goes back to three cards).

Each player must play a card without sending the total higher than 99.
If a player cannot play any card that keeps the total at or below 99, that
player loses a token and the round ends.

The cards are then shuffled and dealt again, and a new round begins.

HOW TO KEEP SCORE / WINNING
Each lost round costs the losing player one token. A player is eliminated when
they lose their third token. The last player with a token remaining is the
winner.

QUICK REFERENCE — card effects
  2,3,5,6,7,8 ....... add face value
  4 ................. reverse direction, +0
  9 ................. pass, +0
  10 ................ subtract 10
  J, Q ............. +10 each (face card = 10 points)
  K ................. set total to 99
  A ................. +1 or +11 (player chooses when played)

NOTES ON VARIATIONS (house rules — only apply these if the players say they
are using them; they are NOT part of the Bicycle rules above):
  - Some groups let a 10 be played as +10 OR -10 at the player's choice.
  - Some groups treat the 3 as a second "pass" card (+0).
  - Some groups allow "stacking" so a player forced out can be saved by a
    reversal. Not in the standard rules.
